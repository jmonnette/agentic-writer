# Article Format Skill

## Purpose

Defines structural and publication conventions for thought leadership articles. Load alongside `personal-voice` for all article writing. This skill governs *structure and format*; `personal-voice` governs *how to write*.

## Publication Context

- **Primary channel**: LinkedIn long-form articles
- **Secondary**: Personal blog / portfolio site
- **Audience**: Engineering leaders, technology executives, consulting buyers, AI practitioners

---

## Article Types and When to Use Each

### Short Articles (600-900 words)
- Single punchy argument. No more than 3 sections.
- Higher density of ultra-short sentences and intentional fragments.
- Opinion/commentary mode — less evidence-heavy than analytical pieces.
- No toolkit or framework sections.
- **Best for**: Timely takes, hot opinions, short provocations, responses to current events.

### Medium Articles (1,200-2,000 words)
- 4-6 sections. Standard argumentative structure.
- Balance evidence (3-5 cited stats) with prescription.
- One framework or model is appropriate; more than one dilutes focus.
- **Best for**: Analytical pieces, "how to" guides, single-concept deep dives.

### Long Articles (2,000-3,500 words)
- 6-8 sections. Include implementation toolkit, maturity model, or phased approach.
- Detailed case studies. Highest statistical density (8-10 cited stats).
- **Best for**: Comprehensive guides, definitive pieces, evergreen reference content.

## Structural Template

### Opening (10%)
- Choose a hook from `personal-voice` patterns (Stakeholder Question, Direct Thesis, Triple-Beat, Historical Contrast, Epigraph, Multi-Question Sequence).
- Establish the tension or problem within the first 3 sentences.
- Thesis visible by end of opening section. Never buried past paragraph 3.

### Body Sections (75-80%)
1. **Landscape section**: Establish current state, the gap, or the misunderstood reality.
2. **Core argument sections**: Each section advances a distinct sub-claim. One claim per section.
3. **Counterargument section**: Address the strongest objection seriously. Never dismiss. Required for medium and long articles.
4. **Prescriptive section**: What to do about it. Required for medium and long articles; optional for short.

### Closing (10-15%)
- Synthesize the core tension — don't summarize what you just said.
- End with an actionable element: diagnostic question, single imperative, or reframed call to action.
- LinkedIn variant: append an Engagement Invitation ("What are you seeing in your organization?").

---

## Header Conventions

- Use declarative claim headers, not generic labels. ("Why AI Coding Tools Don't Solve the Problem" not "AI Tools")
- H2 for major sections; H3 for sub-points only when necessary.
- No more than 2 levels of header depth.
- Headers should do rhetorical work: signal the argument, not just the topic.
- Contrarian/provocative headers are a signature move — use when the section challenges conventional wisdom.

---

## List Conventions

- Never more than 5-6 bullets per list.
- Each bullet is a complete thought, not a fragment.
- Never drop a list without at least one sentence of prose before and after it.
- Numbered lists for sequential steps; bullets for unordered items.
- Lists are support material, not the argument itself. No section should be bullets-only.

---

## Word Count and Brevity

- Agree target word count before drafting.
- Draft within ±10% of target.
- The 15% brevity rule (from `personal-voice`) applies after the draft is complete: the Editor's final pass should land ~15% below the first draft's word count, not below the agreed target.

---

## What to Avoid

- ❌ Titles that promise more than the piece delivers
- ❌ Bullet-only sections with no prose
- ❌ Ending with a listicle (final section should be synthesis prose)
- ❌ More than two frameworks or models in a single article
- ❌ Generic closings that could apply to any article on the topic
- ❌ Orphaned statistics (personal-voice rule applies: every stat gets immediate interpretation)

---

## Quality Checklist (Article-Specific)

Before handoff to Editor:
- [ ] Title matches the actual argument of the piece (not just the topic)
- [ ] Opening hook is specific to this article's argument, not generic
- [ ] Every H2 header does rhetorical work
- [ ] Counterargument addressed seriously (required for medium/long)
- [ ] Prescriptive section present (required for medium/long)
- [ ] Closing has an actionable element
- [ ] No orphaned statistics
- [ ] No more than 2 frameworks or models
- [ ] Final section is prose synthesis, not a list
